# Source check of the FYP research plan (Sept 2026 PDF)

Checked 21 Sep 2026 against the papers themselves (abstracts or full text via PubMed/PMC; where
only the abstract was reachable it says so). Verdicts: **OK** = the report says what the paper
says · **Overstated** = right direction, wrong detail · **Wrong** = the paper says otherwise ·
**Uncited** = the claim is plausible but no source is given.

---

## 1. Bottom line — what must change before this goes further

Five things. The first two matter most because they are the *argument*, not just citations.

| # | What the report says | What is actually the case | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nandi et al. (2023) "explicitly raised whether training cued (exogenous) timing would transfer to uncued (endogenous) timing, **a question no study has yet answered**" | **They answered it.** They tested it and found **no transfer**: "Despite these improvements in exogenous temporal attention, such benefits were not transferred to endogenous attentional ability." | This is *better* for you than what you wrote. It is the strongest possible reason to train endogenous timing directly. Rewrite the gap as: *cued training does not transfer to uncued timing (Nandi 2023), so the uncued component must be trained directly — which no study has done.* |
| 2 | "In every case the pacing cue stayed present" / "The internal clock has never been trained. Every rhythm intervention keeps the cue present." | **Not true as written.** (a) Zanto et al. (2022): in weeks 5–8 of Rhythmicity "the visual cues were presented … then the cues disappeared, and participants continued tapping." (b) Fujioka & Ross (2017): one month of piano training in older adults — "behavioural performance for continuation tapping was improved" vs. untrained controls. | Narrow the claim to what is true: *no study has trained uncued beat-keeping as its target by progressively withdrawing the cue, in a self-administered game.* Cite Fujioka & Ross as evidence the internal clock **is** trainable (it strengthens your case), and Zanto 2022 as the closest precedent (pattern memory, not isochronous continuation; music still playing). |
| 3 | "No rhythm intervention has been delivered without a therapist" | **Wrong.** Zanto 2022 was done "in their own home" on a tablet with adherence monitored by server upload. Rhythm Workers (Bégel et al. 2018) is a self-administered tablet game, used at home in Parkinson's (Dauvergne et al. 2018; Bégel et al. 2022). | Narrow to: *no rhythm intervention in **cognitively impaired** older adults has been delivered without a therapist* — which is what Wang 2024 supports. Or drop "therapist" and say "in a clinic-free, self-administered format targeting the internal clock." |
| 4 | Bangert & Balota: "the difference was largest **after the tone stopped**" | **Not in the paper.** Their task was 12 cued intervals then ~3 minutes of continuation; they analysed continuation only and never compared cued vs uncued. | Say: *their task was essentially a continuation task, so the group differences they found are differences in uncued timing.* That is the true and sufficient version. |
| 5 | Yoo & Kim (2017): "bimanual tapping errors **and their variability** distinguished mild dementia from healthy ageing" | **Half wrong.** Mean synchronisation error did (p = 0.001); variability did **not** (OA vs MD p = 0.882). And the authors are **Kim, Cho & Yoo**. | Fix the author order and drop "variability". |

Everything else is detail — see below — but the five above would be caught by any examiner who
opens the papers.

## 3. The gap claims ("no study has…")

| Claim | Verdict | Nearest counter-example | Safe rewording |
|---|---|---|---|
| The internal clock has never been trained | **False as written** | Fujioka & Ross 2017 (piano, 1 month, continuation improved); Zanto 2022 (cues removed weeks 5–8) | "No study has trained uncued beat-keeping *as its target*, by progressively withdrawing the cue" |
| Every rhythm intervention keeps the cue present | **False** | Zanto 2022 weeks 5–8 | Drop, or "every rhythm intervention in cognitively impaired older adults" |
| No rhythm intervention delivered without a therapist | **False** | Zanto 2022 (home); Rhythm Workers (Bégel 2018; Dauvergne 2018; Bégel 2022, home, Parkinson's) | "…in cognitively impaired older adults" (Wang 2024 supports this) |
| Cued→uncued transfer "a question no study has yet answered" | **False** | Nandi 2023 answered it: no transfer | Turn into your strongest argument (see §1) |
| The Simon conflict has never been placed inside a rhythm | **Holds, with a caveat** | Korolczuk, Burle & Coull 2018 (*Cognition*): temporally **predictable** targets in a Simon task **increase** the conflict effect and impulsive errors. Not an ongoing beat, but the nearest neighbour and it must be cited — it predicts your crossing cost will be measurable | Keep the claim; add Korolczuk as the precedent and the prediction |
| Timing has almost always been measured through one effector | **Holds** | Franz, Zelaznik & Smith 1992 (manual, orofacial, speech timing correlate — in young adults) | Keep; cite Franz for "correlates moderately across effectors" (currently **uncited**) |
| Hand and voice not compared in older adults | **Holds** — no counter-example found | — | Keep |

Also worth citing: Dalla Bella et al. (2017) BAASTA for the synchronisation–continuation method,
CV of ITI, and per-tempo norms — this is what your protocol is actually built on and it is not
in the reference list.

## 7. Suggested replacement for the key paragraph (abstract / §3)

> Rhythm-based training improves attention and memory in cognitively impaired older adults when
> delivered by a therapist (Wang et al., 2024), and a home-based tablet rhythm game has improved
> memory and cued temporal attention in healthy adults aged 60–79 (Zanto et al., 2022; Nandi et
> al., 2023). Those gains did not transfer to uncued timing (Nandi et al., 2023) — yet uncued
> timing is the component that separates the earliest stage of Alzheimer's disease from healthy
> ageing (Bangert & Balota, 2012) and it is trainable (Fujioka & Ross, 2017). No study has made
> uncued beat-keeping the target of training by progressively withdrawing the cue, and no
> rhythm intervention for cognitively impaired older adults has been delivered without a
> therapist.

That version survives someone opening every paper in it.
